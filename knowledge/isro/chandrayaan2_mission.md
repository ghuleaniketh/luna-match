# Chandrayaan-2 Mission Overview & Scientific Objectives

**Mission**: Chandrayaan-2
**Space Agency**: ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation)
**Launch Date**: 22 July 2019
**Orbital Profile**: 100 km x 100 km circular lunar polar orbit
**Primary Science Payloads**:
- Orbiter High Resolution Camera (OHRC)
- Terrain Mapping Camera-2 (TMC-2)
- Imaging Infrared Spectrometer (IIRS)
- Dual Frequency Synthetic Aperture Radar (DFSAR)
- Chandrayaan-2 Large Area Soft X-ray Spectrometer (CLASS)
- Solar X-ray Monitor (XSM)
- Chandra's Atmospheric Composition Explorer-2 (CHACE-2)
- Dual Frequency Radio Science (DFRS) experiment

## Mission Goals
Chandrayaan-2 was designed to expand our understanding of the Moon through detailed topographical, mineralogical, and elemental studies of the lunar surface. The orbiter carries optical, infrared, radar, and X-ray instruments to perform comprehensive multi-modal mapping of the lunar polar and equatorial regions.

## Key Science Objectives
1. **High-Resolution Topography**: Mapping surface morphological features, craters, boulders, and prospective landing sites with sub-meter spatial resolution.
2. **3D Stereo Surface Elevation**: Producing high-precision Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) across diverse terrain.
3. **Mineralogical Mapping**: Identifying surface composition and water-ice/hydroxyl (OH/H2O) signatures in permanently shadowed regions (PSRs) and illuminated polar craters.
4. **Multi-Sensor Data Fusion**: Combining optical panchromatic, stereo elevation, and hyperspectral datasets for scientific analysis.
