# Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) - LROC Narrow Angle Camera (NAC)

**Sensor**: Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera - Narrow Angle Camera (LROC NAC)
**Mission**: Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO)
**Space Agency**: NASA / Arizona State University (ASU)
**Launch Date**: 18 June 2009
**Orbital Altitude**: 50 km (initial science phase) to elliptical polar orbit (30 km x 200 km)

## Technical Specifications
- **Spatial Ground Resolution**: Up to 0.5 meters per pixel from a 50 km orbit altitude.
- **Sensor Configuration**: Two identical Narrow Angle Cameras (NAC-L and NAC-R) producing optical panchromatic swaths.
- **Spectral Band**: Monochrome optical range (400 nm to 750 nm).
- **Combined Swath Width**: 5 km from a 50 km orbit (~10 km at 100 km altitude).
- **Detector**: 5064-pixel line-scan CCD array.

## Purpose & Role in Lunar Data Alignment
1. **Global Control Network**: LROC NAC has produced millions of calibrated, high-resolution lunar surface images over more than a decade, forming the global baseline control standard for modern lunar photogrammetry and target site coordinates.
2. **Cross-Mission Geometric Tie-Points**: Serves as a vital independent reference for validating and co-registering Chandrayaan-2 OHRC and TMC-2 images.
3. **Temporal Surface Change Detection**: Enables comparative analysis of newly formed impact craters, boulder falls, and lander hardware locations on the Moon.
